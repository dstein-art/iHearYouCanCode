1. Getting Started

Everything on this site runs in your web browser — nothing to install. Copy these lines into your index.html file. Load them in this order: p5.js first, then p5.sound, then ProControls. 

<!-- Google icon font (optional — used by ProControls) -->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Material+Symbols+Outlined&display=block" rel="stylesheet">

<!-- p5.js -->
<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/p5@1.11.13/lib/p5.min.js"></script>

<!-- p5.sound (requires p5.js above) -->
<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/p5@1.11.13/lib/addons/p5.sound.min.js"></script>

<!-- ProControls — needed for section 3 -->
<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/dstein-art/proControls4p5js@v0.8.3/ProControls.js"></script>

<!-- Your sketch -->
<script src="sketch.js"></script>
Want a shortcut? We made a p5.js Editor template with everything already set up. Log in, open the template, and click Duplicate to save your own copy. Open template.

2. Add a slider control

ProControls gives you an AnalogSlider — a vertical fader you drag up and down. With no parameters, it appears with default settings: range 0 to 1, auto-placed on the canvas. Call proControlBackground() in draw() to draw the panel behind it.

Try dragging the slider
See the above example in the p5.js Editor

You can also change the slider's starting parameters. Set your own position with x and y, size with width and height, range with min and max, and the starting position of the fader with value.

Try changing x, y, width, height, min, max, or value

3. Group your controls with a panel

A Panel groups controls into a labeled box you can move around the canvas. Create a panel, then call panel.add() to drop in a slider and knobs — ProControls lays them out inside the panel for you.

Drag the panel title bar to move it
See the above example in the p5.js Editor

4. React when the data changes

Wire the panel's onChange callback to run whenever a slider or knob moves. ProControls passes a data object with the current values from every control in the panel. Call openConsolePanel() to open a console window on the canvas — then console.log() inside your callback shows up there as you drag controls.

Move a control and watch the console panel
See the above example in the p5.js Editor

5. Make your first sound

A p5.Oscillator generates a steady tone. Here an IconButton with a radio_button_checked icon toggles the sound on and off — click once to start, click again to stop. Call userStartAudio() on the first click so the browser allows sound.

Click the button to turn sound on and off
See the above example in the p5.js Editor

Why do I need to click first?

Browsers won't play sound until you click something — it's a safety rule. The button calls userStartAudio() for you the first time you press it.

6. Add a second sound, white noise

Layer a second sound source with p5.Noise('white') — a hissy burst of random samples. Update your toggle function so the button starts and stops both the oscillator and the noise together.

Click the button to turn both sounds on and off

Put both sliders on a Panel and wire onChange: dataChange — one callback updates both sound1.amp() and sound2.amp() from the panel's data object.

Turn sound on, then drag the sliders
See the above example in the p5.js Editor

7. Add a XYPad to control the oscillator

Swap the oscillator slider for an XYPad — drag left and right to change sound1.freq(), up and down to change sound1.amp(). The noise slider stays on the panel.
